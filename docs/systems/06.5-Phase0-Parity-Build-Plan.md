## Context — what today's review established

A live playthrough (Chronicle 9, "Wolf Kingdom", Days 1–10) was run in **Phase 0** — a single ChatGPT acting
as the whole engine over 13 hand-saved JSON files — and felt **richer and less restrictive** than the current
**Phase 1** deterministic engine, despite Phase 1 having a superior UI, real persistence, real physics, and no
hallucinated matter. The review of that database + the new GitHub issues (#29–#37) produced **DR-0022**. The
short version: Phase 1 is *sounder but thinner*, and the thinness is fixable **additively**, not by a rewrite.

### Why Phase 0 felt richer (and the corrected reading)
- It said **yes** to any rational action (a "recipe" there is just a *name*; crafting was improvised).
- Every **object was thick** — capabilities, dimensions, provenance (`tools_used`), and a dated
  `modifications` log (the Utility Belt went Revision I→II→III, each adding a tool holder).
- **Corrected principle (user, 2026-08-04):** the goal is **NOT** Phase 0's fuzzy resource pools. Phase 1's
  **exact numerical quantities stay.** The missing piece is **AI reasoning** that says *yes, that's rational*
  and then does the bookkeeping precisely — templates as guides, Architect authors, Auditor validates,
  persistence is numerically accurate. See DR-0022 principles 1–4.
- **Reachability principle (user, 2026-08-04):** an action may use everything **physically in reach** —
  carried, on the ground nearby, **in on-site storage**, and **tools in a nearby rack** — not just carried
  load. One unified model. See DR-0022 principle 2.

#### Multi-zone design — LOCKED (settled with the user; do not re-litigate)

**Reachability is KNOWLEDGE-scoped, not proximity-scoped — and hard-capped at the locality.** An action is a
*goal the chronicle carries out over time*, not a snapshot of arm's reach. A chronicle who knows their own
settlement (built the zones, lived there, or carries a map) fetches what a task needs as part of the act:
"craft a chest box" silently walks to the wood store for planks and the tool shed for the hammer and back.
So the gate is **knowledge/memory (+ a carried map), not distance**, and the cost of the walking is **time**,
folded into the action's duration — never a failure.

**The hard cap (the catch): the local chunk = your settlement/locality.** One action folds in fetching only
within the current chunk. Materials/tools in **another chunk/region are NOT auto-fetched** — the engine never
silently spends years crossing the map, however computable. Beyond the locality is a **journey the player
chooses** (a real MOVE/TRAVEL + haul), and the world says so.

**⚠ Do NOT tighten Layer 1 reachability to the current zone.** (I nearly did; the user stopped me.) Layer 1's
**chunk-scoped reach is CORRECT** — it is exactly the locality cap, justified now by *knowledge* rather than
arm's reach: your whole known settlement is in play, nothing beyond the chunk ever is. Multi-zone must **not**
scope reachability down; it adds naming/organization/navigation *on top* of the unchanged chunk-wide reach.

**So multi-zone is:** many **named zones per chunk** (`chronicle_named_location` keyed per-zone, not the
current one-per-chunk — F8), a **current-zone** pointer for narration and "**go to** the Tool Shed" movement
(a short walk, small time cost, *within* the chunk), and **fetch-aware, location-naming narration**. It is
**not** a reachability restriction.

**Build steps (deterministic, key-free):**
1. Schema: relax `chronicle_named_location`'s one-per-chunk uniqueness → many named zones per chunk; add a
   chronicle `current_zone` (nullable; null = "the chunk at large").
2. Movement: "go to \<zone\>" sets `current_zone`, adds a small fetch/walk time cost. `survey()` names the
   current zone and the other named zones here ("your Tool Shed and Wood Store stand nearby").
3. **Fetch-time:** an action drawing materials from across the settlement adds modest minutes (the walking).
   Keep reachability chunk-wide; only the *duration* reflects the spread.
4. **Cross-chunk reject that names the place:** when a craft's materials are known but in **another** chunk,
   fail with the specific line — *"your planks are at the Wood Store, a day north"* — using the geography /
   named-location knowledge. This is the Layer 5 Part-C reject's specific form (the generic version is shipped;
   this one needs this mechanic to know *where*).
5. **The zone→chunk resolution ripple (the bulk of the work):** if the chronicle's location can be a zone,
   every chunk-keyed query — biome, weather, ecology, `survey()`, drink/wash (`waterInReach`), examination —
   must resolve **zone → parent chunk**. Alternative (lighter): keep `current_location_id` = the chunk always,
   and track `current_zone` as a *separate label* (no location-hierarchy change), so chunk-keyed queries are
   untouched and zones are purely a naming/narration/movement overlay. **Prefer this lighter model unless a
   reason to nest locations emerges** — it avoids the ripple entirely and still delivers the whole feel.

**Already in place:** the chunk cap (Layer 1), the generic missing-materials reject prose (Layer 5 Part C).

## The canonical acceptance example (the basket)

> Reachable (carried + on-site bins/racks): `dry_branch ×5`, `plant_fiber ×5`, `fiber_cordage ×2`, `vine ×3`,
> stone knife in the on-site Tool Shed. Action: *"weave a hand-carry basket with a handle — cut and bend the
> branches to a frame, weave fibre and vine across it, tie the handle with cordage."*

1. **Layer 1** makes all of the above reachable (branches on the ground, cordage in the fibre bin, knife in the rack).
2. **Interpreter** tries existing recipes → `weave_large_basket` (8 fibre + 8 vine) doesn't fit; no "with handle" variant.
3. **Architect** authors `hand_carry_basket_handled` ← `dry_branch ×3 + plant_fiber ×4 + fiber_cordage ×1 + vine ×2`, tool CUTTING, output mass ≤ input mass; attributes `{handle:true, dimensions:…, capabilities:[carry]}`.
4. **Gate** (mass/reachability) → **QA** (plausibility, ≤2 rounds) → `chronicle_tech_discovery` → **Auditor** validates.
5. **Execute** consumes exactly those counts; the basket persists with accurate numerical data and is grab-able later.

When this round-trips with the key on, the thread's core goal is met.

## Risks, unknowns, and the confidence gate

**Where I am NOT yet confident, stated plainly:**

1. **Nothing is built.** Plan-confidence ≠ build-confidence. A playthrough before Layers 1–4b ship will fail as #29–#37 did.
2. **AI-reasoning quality is iterative and key-dependent** — but now has a concrete, *offline* plan. The full engineering (validated-schema output, prompt caching, golden-set eval harness, measured model-tier frontier, loop tuning, dedup, telemetry) is in [runtime-procedure-authoring.md → "AI reasoning quality"](../architecture/runtime-procedure-authoring.md#ai-reasoning-quality--making-it-good-cheap-and-measurable). Key point: the gate makes weak output *rejected, not catastrophic*, and the eval harness makes tuning cheap/auto-scored so the expensive keyed run happens **once, at the end, against an already-tuned pipeline.** Still not one-shot — budget a handful of eval→fix iterations.
3. **Model-tier decision is unmade — and will be made on evidence, not guessed.** The eval harness measures the quality/cost frontier per role (run the Architect on the strongest model for the ceiling, step down until the acceptance suite breaks, pick the cheapest that still passes). Cost aligns with rarity — the expensive Architect/QA fire only on genuine novel authoring, then free after dedup/canonisation — so a strong Architect is affordable. Final tier choice (and subscription tiering) is the user's, informed by the eval numbers.
4. **Latency/cost per novel action.** Authoring is several sequential LLM calls (Interpreter → Architect → QA ≤2 rounds → Auditor). For a playthrough doing many novel things, that is slow and paid **the first time** for each mechanic (then free after canonisation). The loading overlay helps but the wait itself is a UX risk to "engaging."
5. **No live baseline.** Findings are code-read, not observed. Integration tests skip locally (Docker). We must boot the stack and measure current behaviour before/after.

**The confidence gate (do this BEFORE the user's real playthrough):**
- [ ] Ship Layers 1–4b (deterministic) and the quick fixes.
- [ ] Boot the stack; run the **acceptance suite A1–A10** deterministically; record pass/fail as the measured baseline→result.
- [ ] With the operator key, enable the AI switches; run A4/A5/A10 (the authoring cases) end-to-end; **iterate prompts** until they pass and read well.
- [ ] Only when the suite passes and reads richly do we tell the user "play." Until then, the honest status is *in progress, not ready*.

**Bottom line for the next cycle:** do not report "ready" on plan completion. Report "ready" only when the acceptance suite passes on a booted stack — deterministically for A1–A3/A6–A9, and through the tuned pipeline for A4/A5/A10.
