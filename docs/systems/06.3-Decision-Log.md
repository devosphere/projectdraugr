# 06.3 – Decision Log

> **Project Draugr**
>
> *Every important decision should be remembered.*

---

# Purpose

This document records major architectural, gameplay, technical, and creative decisions made throughout the development of Project Draugr.

Its purpose is to preserve the reasoning behind important decisions so future contributors understand **why** a decision was made—not merely **what** was decided.

This document serves as the project's institutional memory.

---

# Decision Rules

Every recorded decision should include:

- Decision ID
- Date
- Category
- Decision
- Reasoning
- Impact
- Status

Once accepted, decisions should not be modified.

If a decision changes, create a new entry that supersedes the previous one.

History should never be erased.

## DR-0001

**Date**

2026-07

**Category**

Vision

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

Project Draugr is fundamentally a **persistent world simulation**, not an AI-first project.

### Reasoning

Early design iterations centered heavily around large-scale artificial intelligence, where nearly every entity would eventually possess its own intelligence.

Although aligned with the long-term vision, this approach made the MVP financially and technically unrealistic.

The project was re-centered around a persistent world simulation.

Artificial intelligence became a future enhancement rather than the project's foundation.

### Impact

- Simplified MVP scope.
- Reduced technical complexity.
- Preserved long-term vision.
- Established "Simulation First" philosophy.

## DR-0002

**Date**

2026-07

**Category**

World Design

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

The world contains **no living human civilization**.

### Reasoning

Traditional RPGs rely on NPC towns and quest hubs.

Project Draugr instead allows civilization to emerge entirely from player actions.

This strengthens persistence and historical continuity.

### Impact

- Every settlement becomes player-created.
- Exploration emphasizes wilderness.
- Civilization becomes emergent gameplay.

## DR-0008

**Date**

2026-07-29

**Category**

Persistence

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

The implementation uses PostgreSQL runtime state plus a separate immutable event archive. Physical objects use UUID identities and transition lifecycle state rather than being deleted.

### Reasoning

The world requires authoritative current state without sacrificing permanent historical continuity. Runtime queries and historical recording have different operational needs, so they must be stored and governed separately.

### Impact

- A database migration owns every schema change.
- `world_object` retains identity through moves, consumption, and destruction.
- Every active object is constrained to one location or owner.
- `world_event` rejects updates and deletes at the database boundary.
- Future adaptive civilization domains are additive migrations, introduced only when the simulation establishes a need.

## DR-0009

**Date**

2026-07-29

**Category**

MVP architecture

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

The MVP adopts the three-layer core-agent architecture: Simulation Agent, Persistent State Architect, and Persistent State Auditor.

### Reasoning

The simulation needs clear accountability for proposing outcomes, committing durable reality, and independently checking consistency. Deterministic rules provide the initial implementation; optional AI services must remain bounded by those same responsibilities.

### Impact

- Simulation logic cannot write persistence directly.
- Schema changes arrive through reviewed Flyway migrations only.
- Auditing remains read-only.
- Future AI API use is an implementation detail behind the Simulation Agent boundary, not authority over world truth.

## DR-0010

**Date**

2026-07-29

**Category**

Narration

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

Narration may express fact-backed immediate perception but may not diagnose Body HUD state, advise the Chronicle, or offer hidden-state hints.

### Reasoning

The narration should remain immersive without rescuing a player who ignores the Body HUD. Sensory atmosphere is part of first-person experience; clinical labels and survival guidance are not.

### Impact

- Narration policy validation rejects advice and explicit internal-state labels.
- The Body HUD remains the authoritative player interface for physiology.
- AI narration, when introduced, receives only a permitted fact packet and passes the same validation.

## DR-0012

**Date**

2026-07-31

**Category**

Persistence

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

The world is ONE shared persistent entity. When a chronicle dies, all `world_object` rows they created or gathered remain in the world unless they naturally decay. The new chronicle spawns at a random location in the same world. Objects, constructions, and literature from previous chronicles are discoverable legacy.

### Reasoning

Permanent death would be meaningless if the world reset with each life. The world's history — built structures, written documents, scattered tools — must persist as the accumulated legacy of all chronicles. A new chronicle finding a dead predecessor's stone archive is not a reward mechanic; it is the natural consequence of a world that never forgets.

### Impact

- `world_object` rows are never deleted for any reason. Lifecycle state transitions to DESTROYED; the row remains.
- New chronicles spawn at a random location, making retrieval of a prior chronicle's legacy a matter of exploration and luck, not a guaranteed restart bonus.
- Literature becomes the most powerful legacy mechanic: knowledge written on stone slabs or bark sheets survives and is readable by any future chronicle who finds it.
- Constructions decay per the existing integrity system but are never hard-deleted.

## DR-0013

**Date**

2026-07-31

**Category**

Technical Architecture

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

Chronicle capability (what a chronicle knows) is per-chronicle and dies with the chronicle. DB schema (which domains exist as tables) is monotonic and global — new domains are added once, for all time, the first time that domain is invented anywhere in the world's history.

### Reasoning

Two kinds of "knowledge" exist in the system and must not be confused. `chronicle_capability_adaptation` rows represent a body's muscle memory and practiced skill — they belong to one life and end with it. Database tables for a domain (e.g. pottery kilns, smithing forges) must exist globally the moment any object of that domain exists in the world, because a future chronicle may find those objects and interact with them. Branching schemas per chronicle would make it impossible for a new chronicle to use a dead chronicle's forge even if they stand in front of it.

### Impact

- When a chronicle dies, their familiarity scores are gone. A new chronicle starts at zero even in a world full of functional infrastructure.
- When a new domain (pottery, smithing, agriculture) is invented for the first time anywhere, the Persistent State Architect adds the required tables ONCE via a Flyway migration. All future chronicles inherit those tables in the schema.
- The distinction: you can FIND a dead chronicle's kiln (world_object row persists). You cannot INHERIT their skill (chronicle_capability_adaptation does not transfer).

## DR-0014

**Date**

2026-07-31

**Category**

Artificial Intelligence

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

Before any AI agent is introduced, six foundation items must exist: (F1) a structured perception/context frame as the AI seam, (F2) tick progression deltas surfaced in that frame, (F3) ATTENTION-scaled world detail, (F4) all death vectors fully wired, (F5) a complete Auditor invariant catalog, (F6) Auditor runtime scheduling and endpoint. Only after F1–F6 are complete will the Simulation Agent (AI narration) be wired as Task #21.

### Reasoning

An AI agent operating on incomplete or inconsistent context will narrate incorrect states, hallucinate missing physiology, and undermine the witness-stance contract. The context frame (F1) is the seam — every AI reads from it. If the seam does not exist yet, adding an AI agent produces noise, not narration. The Auditor (F5/F6) must be complete before any AI can mutate state, because the Auditor is the only safety net.

### Impact

- Task #21 (AI narration) is explicitly blocked until F1–F4 are done.
- The Persistent State Architect is blocked until F7 (domain-creation pattern) and the F8 schema decision are complete. F8 is now decided (DR-0013).
- Build order: F1 → F4 → F2/F3 → F5/F6 → F7 → wire AIs.

## DR-0015

**Date**

2026-07-31

**Category**

Artificial Intelligence

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

Before wiring AI narration (Task #21), the deterministic ecosystem must reach maximum coverage: full species registries across all kingdoms (flora, insects, land wildlife, aquatic, aerial, monsters), behavioral FSM with cascade rules, and a NarrationEngine producing quality witness-stance prose for every intent×outcome combination. AI narration is introduced only after this foundation is complete.

### Reasoning

AI API calls are metered per token. Every action that can be narrated deterministically eliminates a token cost forever. The ecosystem expansion (V39–V46) covers all standard player interactions — gathering flora, encountering wildlife, fishing, tracking, taming — at zero marginal cost. A NarrationEngine ensures the deterministic path produces quality prose. When AI narration is finally wired, 90%+ of player actions already route to the deterministic path, making the AI financially sustainable at any player volume.

### Impact

- Migrations V39–V46 (flora, insects, expanded land wildlife, aerial + aquatic species, monsters, passive encounters, taming, lure/traps) precede Task #21.
- NarrationEngine precedes NarrationRouter.
- AI narration scope is bounded to: death (any cause), monster encounters (MONSTER-category species), WRITE/EDIT_DOCUMENT, OBSERVE with attention=HIGH, bond reaching TAMED stage, first discovery of another chronicle's objects, and action text > 300 chars with a non-trivial intent.

## DR-0016

**Date**

2026-07-31

**Category**

Artificial Intelligence / Narration

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

The NarrationEngine generates complete, factually correct, witness-stance prose for every action. When the AI narration layer is invoked, it receives the existing backend prose plus the PerceptionFrame and contributes one to two sentences of atmospheric or sensory flavor. It does not generate narration from scratch.

### Reasoning

Full AI generation from a raw PerceptionFrame costs approximately 800 output tokens per call. Refinement of existing prose costs approximately 60 output tokens — roughly 13× cheaper per meaningful moment. The backend prose covers all facts (species, wound severity tier, behavioral outcome, location). The AI adds only what deterministic code cannot produce: texture, dread, atmosphere, sensory immediacy. This architecture also means the game ships with complete, correct narration on day one. AI is the upgrade layer, not the dependency.

### Impact

- NarrationEngine is a first-class production system, not a placeholder fallback.
- The PerceptionFrame gains a `backendNarration` field which the AI refinement call receives alongside the structured frame.
- AI output is bounded to one or two sentences maximum.
- The NarrationRouter (DR-0017) governs which actions receive AI refinement.
- If the Claude API is unavailable, the game continues functioning with backend narration unchanged.

## DR-0017

**Date**

2026-07-31

**Category**

Artificial Intelligence / Performance

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

A `NarrationRouter` classifies every resolved action as `DETERMINISTIC` or `AI_REFINE` before any API call is attempted. The router reads signals already present in the PerceptionFrame: intent, attention level, outcome, sinceLastFrame delta count, species category (MONSTER vs WILDLIFE vs none), and action text length. DETERMINISTIC actions return the NarrationEngine prose unchanged. AI_REFINE actions proceed to the Claude API refinement call.

### Reasoning

Without a router, every player action incurs an API cost regardless of narrative significance. A player gathering clay twenty times in succession produces the same backend prose each time — no AI refinement is warranted. The router is the financial control layer that preserves sustainability at scale.

### Impact

- **Always DETERMINISTIC:** EQUIP, UNEQUIP, DROP, ADD_FUEL, DESIGNATE, MARK, GATHER_PLANT, FELL_TREE, FISH (terse), COLLECT_INSECTS, LURE, SET_TRAP, TRACK (no sign), any intent with attention=LOW and trivial outcome.
- **Always AI_REFINE:** death (any cause), any MONSTER-category species encounter, WRITE / EDIT_DOCUMENT, OBSERVE with attention=HIGH, bond reaching TAMED stage, first discovery of another chronicle's objects.
- **Conditional AI_REFINE:** action text > 300 characters with non-trivial intent; sinceLastFrame carries two or more state changes; CONFRONT with wound severity ≥ 35.
- The router is pure Java with no DB dependency — testable without Docker.

## DR-0018

**Date**

2026-07-31

**Category**

Simulation

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

Wildlife and monster behavioral intelligence is implemented as an expanded finite state machine (FSM) with cascade rules between species. No language model is used for behavioral decisions. The FSM must produce emergent-feeling behavior — pack coordination, prey awareness cascades, scavenger attraction, seasonal aggression, ambush exemptions — through deterministic tick-based state transitions.

### Reasoning

AI-driven behavior is expensive, non-deterministic, and impossible to audit for correctness. A well-designed FSM with inter-species cascade rules produces behavior that appears intelligent without any token cost. Pack wolves coordinating, deer becoming alert when wolves hunt two chunks away, ravens converging on a fresh carcass — these all emerge from state machine rules evaluated on the ecology tick. Players cannot distinguish this from AI-driven behavior, and the simulation remains auditable and reproducible.

### Impact

- `wildlife_population.behavior_state` expands from 6 values to include STALKING, FEEDING, DRINKING, MIGRATING, NESTING, TERRITORIAL, PACK_HUNT.
- `WildlifeSimulationService` gains inter-species cascade evaluation: predator state changes trigger prey awareness shifts in adjacent chunks.
- Ambush hunter flag on wildcat and adder suppresses the ALERT signal before a passive attack.
- Seasonal modifiers applied per tick: winter → bear SHELTERING, autumn ungulates → TERRITORIAL.
- Scavenger cascade: any carcass created → raven/vulture/jackal populations shift to FORAGING toward that chunk.

## DR-0019

**Date**

2026-07-31

**Category**

Simulation / Narration

**Status**

Accepted

### Decision

All action text passes through an `ActionInputClassifier` before intent classification. Inputs classified as OBSCENE, NONSENSICAL, or PHYSICALLY_IMPOSSIBLE are intercepted and returned with deterministic narrator prose. They never reach the intent dispatcher, never consume AI tokens, and never produce a `chronicle_action` row.

### Reasoning

Players will submit sexual actions, profanity-directed actions, complete gibberish, and physically impossible commands. This is not an edge case — it is a certainty. Sending these to an AI narrator wastes tokens and produces responses that may be inappropriate or confusing. The simulation is a physics engine. The world responds to physical reality, not to sentiment or biology. A deterministic "the world continues without comment" response is both cheaper and more thematically consistent than any AI-generated reply.

### Impact

- `ActionInputClassifier.java` runs as the first step in `ChronicleActionService.resolve()`, before `classifyIntent()`.
- Classification categories: `VALID`, `OBSCENE`, `NONSENSICAL`, `PHYSICALLY_IMPOSSIBLE`.
- Classified inputs return an `ActionResult` with no `chronicle_action` write, no tick advance, and deterministic narration from the `InputResponseLibrary`.
- The narrator's tone for these responses is: flat, realistic, indifferent. The world is not offended. The world does not react. It simply continues.
- No Body HUD changes. No time passes. The moment is not recorded in history.

# Superseded Decisions

When a decision changes, do not delete the original.

Instead:

- Change its status to **Superseded**.
- Record the new decision.
- Reference the previous Decision ID.

This preserves the project's design history.

---

# Design Philosophy

Changing a decision is not considered failure.

Projects evolve.

The important principle is that every significant change is documented together with the reasoning behind it.

Future contributors should never need to guess why the project was designed the way it is.

---

# Final Principle

Project Draugr preserves not only the world's history, but also its own development history.

Just as every Chronicle leaves a mark upon the world, every important design decision leaves a mark upon the project.

Nothing meaningful should be forgotten.
