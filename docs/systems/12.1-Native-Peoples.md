# 12.1 – Native Peoples: Specification Cards

> **Project Draugr** — epic #109, stories #110–#116, task #154, decision DR-0024.

A native people is not a monster with dialogue. Each one here is specified in full before anything of it is placed in a world, and each is placed only after that specification is accepted (DR-0024). #115 caps the first release at **two** cultures.

| people | status | where it lives in the code |
|---|---|---|
| **Reedkin** | **Placed** (V346). First slot. | `cognition_profile`, `NativeCommunityService.seedPeoples`, contact, trade, conduct, territory |
| **Grovebound** | **Placed** (V366). Second slot, and the release cap is now met. | the same services, plus `seedGrovebound`, `made_goods`, and two refusals in `cognition_profile` |
| Stonekin | Deferred (DR-0024). Waits on cave-interior settlement. | — |
| Centaur | Candidate. Card below. Not placed. | — |

The rules every card must answer, from #154 and #81: biology, personhood, habitat, settlement needs, roles, property, language and etiquette, consent, tradeable physical goods, conflict consequences, remains policy, and a source-to-use chain for everything they would make or trade.

What any placed people gets for free, because the systems are shared:

- **Protection by class (V344).** `PEOPLE` makes domestication, restraint and harvest impossible by constraint, and taming and the carcass harvest read the same columns.
- **A community that lives (V345).** Real stores, seasonal gathering, hunger, closing up and moving on, and an append-only history.
- **Contact, trade, memory and territory (#112–#114, #211).**

A new people is therefore mostly a *specification* and a *seed*: its species row and profile, its staple and made goods, where its settlements go, and what its members do.

## Reedkin

The full card is DR-0024 in `06.3-Decision-Log.md`. In summary: a web-footed, fur-coated marsh-and-river-isle people. Fish is their staple, and reed, withy, cordage and bone make up their material culture. Elders govern and a headsperson speaks for the isle. At most two isles per world, on freshwater marsh.

## Centaur (candidate)

**Biology.** Large, four-legged and hoofed, with an upright torso, arms and hands; warm-blooded and a grazer-browser by gut, but able to eat meat. One foal at a time, born after a long gestation; slow to mature, long-lived. Heavy: a centaur cannot cross soft marsh or steep rock, and cannot use a ladder, a narrow bridge or a low door. The **size is the whole ecology**: open grassland and the edge of forest are the only ground that can carry them.

**Personhood (would be PEOPLE).** Spoken language and a rich gesture-and-stance language for across open ground. Named individuals, a herd-band kin model, and oral history kept by the oldest.

**Habitat and settlement.** GRASSLAND, with the forest edge in winter. Mobile: a band moves between seasonal camps on a circuit, which makes it the only people whose home is a *route*, not a place. This is the reason it waits. It needs the travel-cost topology (#622) and a seasonal-camp movement model in `NativeCommunityService` (today MOVING is a state, not a journey).

**Roles.** Band-leader, herders of the circuit's water and grazing, hunters, makers (bows, bowstrings, hide), foals.

**Property and goods.** Hide and hardened leather, bows and bowstrings (`bow_stave`, `bowstring_sinew`), horn, and dried meat. Their need is iron-free edges and fine cordage. Every good exists in the catalogue today.

**Etiquette and consent.** An outsider approaches in the open where they can be seen from far off, and never from downwind. Mounting a centaur is **never** permitted: they are persons, and V344's `restraint_prohibited` already makes riding one impossible.

**Conflict.** They retaliate as a band and can pursue across open ground faster than a Chronicle can walk. A grudge follows the circuit.

**Remains.** A person's: `remains_protected`. The band returns its dead to a place on the circuit, which would be a protected site under #211.

**What placing it needs first.** Band movement along a seasonal circuit, band territory as a moving region, and pursuit.

## Grovebound (placed, V366)

> **Placed as the second culture.** Both systems this card named as missing were built: fire as a threat to a
> settlement (#111/#662) and felling recognised as the gravest encroachment (#211/V365). The card stands as
> written; what follows records how it was placed.
>
> - **One grove to a world** — a rooted people is a place, not a population — on forest the world already calls old
>   timber where it can, never on a monster's ground, six chunks clear of any other people's home.
> - **Eight of them** in two kin households: a speaker, an elder, foragers, makers and a child. `dried_mushroom` is
>   their staple; `bark_sheet`, `pine_resin`, `herbal_poultice` and `fiber_cordage` are what their hands make.
> - **They refuse two things, and the refusals are data**: `companionship_eligible FALSE` (they do not leave
>   the grove with strangers) and `community_membership FALSE` (nobody becomes grovebound). They are the first people
>   to answer NO to any of V344's eligibility columns, which is what those columns were for.
> - **No service was taught about them.** Contact, trade, agreements, claims, territory, succession, remains, news,
>   membership and shelter all read the community row (DR-0026), so placing them was a migration and a placement
>   rule. `made_goods` moved out of a Java constant onto the row to make that true.
> - **Still owed them:** their bark-records. This card names the literature system as their natural partner, and a
>   grove that keeps its memory in marks cut on bark should have documents in it a Chronicle can be shown.

### The card as accepted

**Biology.** Slow-growing, long-lived woodland people of bark-like skin and fibrous limbs. Warm-blooded, but they live largely on nuts, mast, fungi and sap. Few young, rarely born. They tolerate shade and cold and fear fire above everything.

**Personhood (would be PEOPLE; possibly SOVEREIGN for the eldest).** Slow speech and a language of marks cut in bark, so they are the one people with a written record: a natural partner for the literature system. Their memory is long, their decisions slow, and their grudges longer.

**Habitat and settlement.** Old-growth TEMPERATE_FOREST, at the "Old-growth timber" sites the world already places. Settled: the grove is the home, and it is the trees themselves, which makes felling in their territory the gravest encroachment (#211).

**Roles.** Keepers of the grove, gatherers, the eldest (sovereign-like), makers of bark-cloth and resin goods.

**Property and goods.** Nuts and mast, dried mushrooms, resin and pitch, bark sheets and bark cordage, and medicinal herbs (`agrimony_herb`, `herbal_poultice`). Their need is stone tools they will not make themselves, since they will not break the living rock.

**Etiquette and consent.** Carry no fire into the grove, cut nothing, and wait to be noticed. That can take a day.

**Conflict.** They are slow to anger and do not forget. They answer with closure and the forest itself: paths lost, forage withheld. Fire in their grove is war.

**Remains.** A person's: the body is returned to the grove.

**What placing it needs first.** Tree-felling in their territory recognised as encroachment of the gravest kind (#211 extension), fire as a settlement threat (#114's remaining damage-and-fire work), and the literature system reading their bark-records.

## The second slot

The choice between **stonekin**, **centaur** and **grovebound** is left open deliberately. Each needs one system that does not yet exist:

| candidate | missing system |
|---|---|
| Stonekin | cave-interior settlement, and stone/ore/lime chains specified for them |
| Centaur | band movement along a seasonal circuit, and pursuit |
| ~~Grovebound~~ | ~~felling as grave encroachment, and fire damage to settlements~~ — **both built; placed in V366** |

The first of those systems to be built decides which people comes second. That kept the order grounded in what the world could carry rather than in preference, and it decided this one: fire damage to settlements arrived with #662, felling as the gravest encroachment with V365, and the grovebound went in.

**The cap is now met.** #115 allows two cultures in the release and the world has two. A third needs that cap revisited, not just a card and a migration — and the register (#120) refuses the community until somebody does.

## Stonekin enclaves (candidate, #118)

**Biology.** Large-bodied, heavy-boned, low-light-adapted people. Slow to tire and slow to move; they eat less often than their size suggests and drink a great deal. Few young, raised long. Eyes that work in a lamplit tunnel are poor in full sun, so work above ground is early-morning and evening work.

**Personhood (would be PEOPLE).** Spoken language, and a tapping speech through rock that carries where a voice does not. Named individuals, lineage kin, and the longest collective memory of any candidate: an enclave remembers a wrong for generations, which makes reconciliation with them slow rather than impossible.

**Habitat and settlement.** CAVE and MOUNTAIN, at limestone faces, scree slopes, quarry margins and warm springs. Settled and hard to move: a hall is cut, not pitched. That is the reason they wait — a cave-interior settlement needs ventilation, drainage and a light source to be a home rather than a hole, and none of those are modelled.

**Roles.** Mason, quarrier, kiln-and-lime worker, tunnel surveyor, keeper of the memory, and apprentices who are apprenticed for years.

**Property and goods.** Cut stone, lime, quicklime and mortar, ore and smelted metal, clay and fired ware, and lamps that burn fat. What they want from outside is what they cannot grow: hide, fibre, dried food, resin, and news. Every one of those exists in the catalogue.

**Etiquette and consent.** An outsider waits at the threshold and does not carry a light into a hall uninvited. Striking their rock without leave — quarrying inside their ground — is the offence a stonekin enclave punishes most heavily, and it maps exactly onto #211's territory rules.

**Conflict.** They do not pursue. They close the ground: galleries collapsed, water diverted, the way in gone. A grudge lasts a lifetime and is inherited.

**Remains.** A person's: `remains_protected`. They are laid in the rock they worked, and the place is a grave.

**What placing it needs first.** Cave-interior settlement (a home inside the rock, with air, water and light), and the stone/ore/lime chains specified as theirs rather than as generic quarry output. **A sapient stonekin is never a troll carcass**: V344's constraints already make that impossible, and the instinctive cave troll stays a separate PRIMAL species.

## Harpy aerie clans (candidate, #118)

**Biology.** Winged, hollow-boned, light for their height; they cannot carry much and cannot go far carrying it. Eggs and hatchlings, on ledges, in a season. They feel cold and wet badly, and a wet wing does not fly.

**Personhood (would be PEOPLE).** Voice and call over distance, which is what a cliff people needs. The harpy already in the catalogue is classified SOCIAL by behaviour (V344) and stays so: this candidacy is for an *aerie clan*, and activating it means writing a people, not reclassifying a flock.

**Habitat and settlement.** HIGHLAND and MOUNTAIN cliffs, gorges, and ruins nobody can walk into. A vertical settlement: ledges, weather screens, hauled stores, hatchling ledges above the reach of anything that climbs.

**Roles.** Aerie-speaker, wind-scout, collector (they reach ground no walker reaches), featherworker, hatchling-guard, elder.

**Property and goods.** Feathers and down, cordage and woven line, dried meat and fish, and things collected from places nobody else can get to — which is what makes trade with them worth a climb. They want what cliffs do not give: grain, salt, worked edges, and hide.

**Etiquette and consent.** Approach is by a named route and never from above. A gift is left on a ledge and looked at before anyone comes down.

**Conflict.** They do not hold ground; they watch it. Their answer to a wrong is to take something and be a long way off with it, which is the **theft behaviour the world already has, redesigned as custom**: it is how a clan fines an outsider, not a species trait.

**Remains.** A person's. Their dead go off the cliff into the gorge, and the place is remembered.

**What placing it needs first.** Vertical settlement (a home a walker cannot reach, and stores that are hauled), and flight as a real constraint — carrying limits, weather, and no service flights for a Chronicle.

## Saurian kin (candidate, #118)

**Biology.** Ectothermic, scaled, semi-aquatic. Their day is shaped by heat: they bask to work, and in cold they slow to nothing. Eggs in a warm nest mound, guarded. That is the whole of their ecology and the whole reason they wait.

**Personhood (would be PEOPLE).** Language, tools, kin lines, and a long patience.

**Habitat and settlement.** WETLAND and warm slopes, at basking shelves and nest mounds. Their settlement follows the sun across a season rather than staying put.

**Roles.** Nest-keeper, basker-watch, fisher, knapper, and elders who decide in the warm part of the day.

**Property and goods.** Fish and turtle, hide and scale, knapped stone, and reed. They want fire-kept warmth in the cold months, which is the one thing they cannot easily make for themselves and the best thing a Chronicle can offer them.

**Etiquette and consent.** Never step on a nest mound. Business is done in the warm hours; a visitor who arrives at dawn is not refused, they are simply not answered yet.

**Conflict.** Slow, and patient. They wait for the cold to take an enemy who has to keep moving.

**Remains.** A person's.

**What placing it needs first.** Body temperature as a real state for a people (their working hours, their winter), and nest-mound sites the world places and protects.

## Deepstone oracle (candidate, #118, Tier IV)

**Biology.** One being, in one chamber, older than any community in the world. It does not travel, does not reproduce in any season anyone has seen, and eats little and rarely.

**Personhood (would be SOVEREIGN).** Language, and a memory that goes back past every people's own. **Not omniscient**: it knows what has come to it, which is what has been *carried* to it — so what it knows of the surface is what visitors have told it, and it can be wrong, and it can be lied to.

**Habitat.** A single CAVE chamber, with its own water. The chamber is the being's territory and its whole world.

**Property.** What has been brought to it over centuries, kept where it lies. Death does not make a hoard ownerless (#122): the claims on it are whatever the world says they are.

**Etiquette and consent.** You bring something. You ask one thing. You are answered or you are not.

**Conflict.** It does not fight. It stops answering, and tells the next visitor who you are.

**What placing it needs first.** An individually authored being with its own dialogue of *knowledge*, not quests — which means a knowledge-exchange model the game does not have yet, and the discipline to keep it fallible.

## Elder groveheart (candidate, #118, Tier IV)

**Biology.** A rooted being: it does not move at all, and its life is measured in centuries. It feeds as a tree feeds, and its injuries are a tree's injuries.

**Personhood (would be SOVEREIGN).** Slow speech, in a language the grovebound (below) can translate and a stranger cannot. It is a *place* as much as a person, which is its whole interest: an offence against it is an offence against the ground.

**Habitat.** One old-growth TEMPERATE_FOREST grove, which is its body's territory.

**Conflict.** It cannot chase, strike, or close a gate. What it can do is everything the forest does: paths lost, forage withheld, and every creature that lives in the grove made unwilling.

**What placing it needs first.** Felling within a territory recognised as the gravest encroachment (#211 extension), and a being whose responses are *ecological* rather than personal — the one candidate whose whole conflict model is the habitat itself.

## Goblin bands (candidate, #119, Tier IIb)

**Biology.** Small, quick, short-lived, many young. They eat anything and get by on less than anything else that talks. Poor in open ground and excellent in broken ground.

**Personhood (would be PEOPLE).** Language, kin bands, and a working material culture of repair: almost nothing they own was made new by them, and almost everything they own works.

**Habitat and settlement.** Forest-edge burrows, scrub ravines, ANCIENT_RUIN margins, quarry edges, low caves. Hidden, but physical: a burrow is dug and smells of smoke, and a Chronicle who looks can find it.

**Roles.** Band chief, kin head, scout, trapmaker, cache-keeper, young, elder, and the outsider a band tolerates.

**Property and goods.** Caches — that is their whole economy. Scavenged and repaired tools, traps, cordage, hides, dried food, and whatever the last ruin gave up. They want edges, salt and rope, and they will trade knowledge of ground nobody else has walked.

**Etiquette and consent.** They deal from cover and at a distance first. A band will bargain before it fights, and will fight rather than be followed home.

**Conflict.** Theft from a cache is the gravest thing you can do to a goblin band, because the cache is the winter. Their answer is traps, night visits, and moving the camp — never a pitched fight, which they lose.

**Remains.** A person's. **No band is hostile by name**: what they are to a Chronicle comes out of what has passed between them, like every other people.

**What placing it needs first.** Caches as real hidden stores that can be found and robbed (#211 property, extended to a store that is not a building), and shifting camps — a settlement that moves without being a migration.

## Ogre lineages (candidate, #119, Tier IIb–III)

**Biology.** Very large, very strong, enormous appetite and thirst. Solitary or in a family; a group of ogres eats a valley empty, which is why there is never a village of them.

**Personhood (would be PEOPLE).** Language and agency, in ones and twos rather than institutions. A lineage's memory is a family's memory: one grudge, held closely.

**Habitat and settlement.** Forest and mountain edge, caves with water, highland passes. A homestead: one heavy shelter, a fire, and stores sized for the body that eats from them.

**Property and goods.** Heavy tools nobody else can swing, hide and bone, firewood by the cartload, and large stores. What they want is food, and what they can honestly sell is **work**: an ogre moves and lifts what a team of oxen would.

**Etiquette and consent.** They negotiate boundaries and they keep bargains exactly — the exact words, not the intent. Deception is the offence they punish hardest.

**Conflict.** Direct and decisive, and rare: fighting is expensive for a body that must eat for it.

**Remains.** A person's: **never a tameable pack animal and never a material source while living** — V344's constraints already refuse both.

**What placing it needs first.** Calorie load as a real constraint on a settlement's placement (an ogre homestead must sit where it can be fed), and paid heavy work as an agreement a Chronicle can actually make — which #113's V353 now provides.

## Cyclops households (candidate, #119, Tier III)

**Biology.** Large-bodied, single-eyed, long-lived, with the depth perception that follows from one eye: superb at what is in their hands, poor at what is thrown at them from a distance.

**Personhood (would be PEOPLE).** Language, households rather than a state, and stone knowledge kept in the family.

**Habitat and settlement.** Mountain valleys, cave mouths, quarry and lime country, highland springs. A household is one built place with a work-yard, and it stays.

**Roles.** Householder, stoneworker, kiln-keeper, and children who learn by doing.

**Property and goods.** Large-scale stone and clay work nobody else in the world can make: querns, troughs, lintels, kiln ware. They want metal, fibre, dried food and safe passage. This is the candidate whose **trade is a reason to travel**, which is the argument for activating it.

**Etiquette and consent.** Business at the work-yard, not in the house. Slow trust, and a contract is a contract — specialised work agreed in advance, never a smith on demand.

**Conflict.** Territorial about the work-site and the water. They throw what they can lift, and they do not pursue.

**Remains.** A person's.

**What placing it needs first.** Large stone goods that are worth the journey (a quern a Chronicle cannot make, and a cart or a boat to move it), and a contracted-work agreement longer than a day — #113's agreements are the shape, with a term and a delivery.

## Orc communities (candidate, #119, Tier III)

**Biology.** Strong, heavy-framed, fast to mature, ordinary appetites for their size. Nothing about them is unusual; that is the point of the card.

**Personhood (would be PEOPLE).** Full: language, households, craft, defence, custom, and politics that differ between communities. **No species-wide alignment.** A clan that has been raided becomes a clan that raids; a clan left alone becomes a clan that trades.

**Habitat and settlement.** GRASSLAND, forest edge and highland routes, in built settlements with fields, stock and a wall — the most ordinary settlement any candidate would bring, and the one that most needs #115's caution, because it is nearest to "a human town".

**Roles.** Clan chief, elders, smith and craft leaders, warden, trade speaker, healer, kin heads, apprentices.

**Property and goods.** Everything a settled people has: grain, stock, metal, leather, cloth, weapons. They are the only candidate who could **buy** as much as sell.

**Etiquette and consent.** Announced approach, a speaker, and formal agreements — the whole of #112 and #113 as a people use them rather than as a stranger's first contact.

**Conflict.** Organised, proportionate, and endable: they fight a war and then they end it, which is what makes reconciliation possible where a grudge-holding people gives none.

**Remains.** A person's, with their own burial custom.

**What placing it needs first.** Fields and stock at a settlement's scale (their economy is agriculture, which the community model does not yet run), and a second-people rule — activating an orc clan makes the world a world with *two* organised peoples in it, and #115 caps the release at two for exactly that reason.

## What these cards are for

None of the above is placed. The register (`native_candidate`, V355, #120) holds each of them with six activation reviews, and the database refuses a community for any species whose candidacy is not ACTIVE. A card here is the first of those reviews written down; the rest are done in the migration that seeds the people, and the migration fails if any is left undone.

Note that fire damage to settlements now exists (#111, PR #662): a village can be burnt and is mended or rebuilt. That was half of what the grovebound are missing. Felling as grave encroachment is the other half.

## The candidate register (#120, V355)

The candidate register is the `native_candidate` table. It holds every native creature and people the world *might* hold: 29 at V355, each with a proposed tier and cognition class, the ground it belongs to, and the form its home would take. The register may grow freely. It is the list, not the world.

**The gate is enforced by the schema:**

- A candidate becomes `ACTIVE` only when all six activation reviews are marked done: tier and interactions, ecology, body and materials, home and footprint, social rules, and actions and tests. It must also name the catalogue species it became. The `active_means_reviewed` constraint refuses anything less.
- A community can be founded only for a species whose candidate is `ACTIVE`. This is the `native_community_must_be_an_active_candidate` trigger, and it applies on top of V345's rule that the species must be a people.
- No more than two candidates can be activated in one release. A constraint cannot compare rows from different releases, so `NativeCandidateRegisterIntegrationTest` enforces this by counting `activated_in` per migration.

**To bring a candidate into the world:** add its species and cognition profile, write its card here, then do each review and mark it done in the same migration that seeds its first community. If a review is left undone, the migration fails. It does not quietly produce a people nobody finished.
